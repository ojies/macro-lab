# macro-lab · Europe module (the stagnation pole)

The UK and the euro area: aging, low-productivity, high-debt advanced economies — plus the euro's
defining structural fragility. The third pole of macro-lab.

Run (from `debt_cycle/`): `uv run python europe/europe_stagnation.py`

## What the model finds
- **Stagnation:** growth ground down from ~4% (2000) to ~0–1.4% (2025); **Germany in outright recession** (−0.9% 2023, −0.5% 2024) as the 2022 energy shock hit its industrial model — a terms-of-trade blow the energy-exporting US escaped.
- **The euro's fragility gauge — the BTP/OAT–Bund spread** (Italy/France 10y minus the German Bund): the market price of *monetary union without fiscal union*. It blew out to **3.99pp (Italy, 2012)** in the sovereign crisis, prompting Draghi's "whatever it takes"; it sits **structurally elevated** today (Italy ~1.0pp, France ~0.76pp — the latter on recent fiscal-political strain).
- **The UK-vs-periphery split:** the UK has its **own currency + central bank** (the BoE unilaterally halted the Sep-2022 gilt/LDI crisis) — its risk is inflation/currency, like a smaller US. The euro **periphery** (Italy ~137% debt) issues debt in a currency it **cannot print** — its risk is categorically closer to EM fragility, contained only by the ECB's conditional backstops (OMT, PEPP, TPI).
- **Diagnosis:** a slow **"Japanification"** — aging, a widening productivity gap vs the US (Draghi: ~70% of the per-capita gap is productivity), high debt, and too little growth to inflate it away. High income, low momentum.

## Where Europe sits among the poles
Between the US (own reserve currency, vast space) and Nigeria (no space): the **UK** is a smaller US (own-currency, can debase/backstop); the **euro periphery** is structurally closer to EM fragility — same currency, no shared fiscal backstop, its risk priced daily in the Bund spread.
