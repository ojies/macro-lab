## PART A — GHANA 2022-2025: THE CLOSEST RECENT AFRICAN ANALOGUE

Ghana is the most useful analogue for Nigeria because it is a Sub-Saharan, commodity-exporting (gold/cocoa/oil), formerly "frontier-darling" economy that hit a debt wall in the same global cycle (post-COVID + Russia-Ukraine commodity/rate shock), then ran an **IMF-anchored, orthodox-tightening** stabilisation. The crucial *difference* from Nigeria: Ghana **defaulted and restructured** (domestic + external), mechanically cutting its debt stock; Nigeria has **not**.

### A.1 Crisis profile — the dashboard

| Field | Value | Flag |
|---|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Loss of Eurobond market access from Nov 2021; pre-existing public debt ~93% of GDP and interest/revenue ratio ~70-100%; COVID fiscal blowout + 2022 Russia-Ukraine food/energy/rate shock; reserves collapsing to ~2 weeks of import cover | [SOURCED] |
| **Debt currency mix** | Roughly half domestic (cedi) / half external (FX). Restructured perimeter: ~GH¢137bn domestic bonds (~US$10bn-equiv), US$13bn Eurobonds, ~US$5.4bn bilateral | [SOURCED] |
| **Peak inflation** | **54.1% (Dec 2022)** — a 2-decade high | [SOURCED] |
| **Depreciation** | Cedi ~**45-50%+** vs USD in 2022 (worst-performing currency globally for stretches of 2022) | [SOURCED] |
| **Default — domestic** | **DDEP** launched Dec 2022, completed Feb 2023: old bonds swapped for new with lower coupons + longer maturities; **NPV loss ~20-40%**; pensions/individuals initially included then **exempted after public protest** | [SOURCED] |
| **Default — external** | **19-20 Dec 2022**: suspended service on most external debt (Eurobonds, commercial, eligible bilateral). Eurobond restructuring **completed Oct 2024**: **37% nominal haircut (~US$5bn)**, coupons capped ~5%, **98%+ creditor consent**. Bilateral handled under **G20 Common Framework** via Official Creditor Committee (~US$5.4bn) | [SOURCED] |
| **Household real-wage / poverty hit** | **~850,000 pushed into poverty in 2022** (World Bank). Minimum wage +10% vs ~53% price rise → **real minimum wage ~ -43% in 2022**. Households depleted savings/sold assets; electricity tariff hikes; poorest (food ~70% of budget) hit hardest | [SOURCED] |
| **IMF program** | **17 May 2023: US$3bn, 3-year ECF** (SDR 2.242bn, 304% of quota). Conditions: debt restructuring (prior action), fiscal consolidation to a primary surplus, monetary tightening (MPR raised to 29-30%), reserve accumulation, no central-bank deficit financing, structural/revenue reforms | [SOURCED] |
| **How it's resolving** | Inflation **54%→23.5% (Jan 2025)→5.4% (Dec 2025)→~3.2% (Mar 2026)**. Cedi **appreciated ~40% vs USD in 2025**. GDP **+6.1% (9M 2025)** — fastest since 2019. Public debt **61.8%→45.0% of GDP (Dec 2024→Oct 2025)**. **Triple ratings upgrade** (S&P to B-). Reserves ~**5.7 months** import cover. **Fifth ECF review completed Dec 2025** (all targets met) | [SOURCED] |
| **Recovery time (macro)** | ~**3 years** trough-to-stabilisation (2022 → 2025) | [ESTIMATED] |
| **Recovery time (household real income)** | Still closing the gap; full recovery of pre-crisis real wages likely **~4-6 years from the 2022 trough** (i.e. ~2026-2028), given the ~43% real-wage hole and only-now-positive real rates | [ESTIMATED] |

### A.2 Beautiful vs Ugly verdict — Ghana

**Verdict: an UGLY ENTRY resolving into a BEAUTIFUL (orderly) DELEVERAGING.** [PROVISIONAL]

- **Ugly elements:** an actual default (domestic *and* external), a brutal real-wage/poverty shock, savers/pensioners haircut, a deep confidence collapse.
- **Beautiful elements:** the *combination* that drives orderly deleveragings was present and sustained — (i) a credible nominal anchor (IMF + aggressive BoG tightening + fiscal primary-surplus consolidation), (ii) debt relief that *mechanically* cut debt/GDP (61.8%→45%), and (iii) disinflation without a prolonged output collapse (growth rebounded to 6%+). Inflation fell ~50 points in ~3 years, the currency *reversed* (rare — a +40% appreciation year), and Ghana regained market access/ratings.
- **Net:** Ghana is tracking toward the *good* tail. The default was not the catastrophe — done cleanly, with IMF cover and high creditor consent, it *accelerated* the deleveraging by resetting the debt stock and the cost of capital.

## PART B — CROSS-CASE SYNTHESIS / BASE RATES

Comparator set: **Argentina** (serial; heterodox→orthodox under Milei 2024-25), **Turkey** (heterodox 2021-23→orthodox pivot), **Egypt** (2022-24, no default, external windfall), **Ghana** (2022-25, default + IMF), **Nigeria 1980s-2005** (lost two decades, SAP, serial Paris Club, 2005 debt relief), plus the **Reinhart-Rogoff** and **IMF** evidence base.

### B.1 The comparator dashboard

| Case | Policy regime | Default? | External support / windfall | Peak inflation | Outcome / time to household recovery | Beautiful vs Ugly |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ghana 2022-25** | Orthodox + IMF ECF | **Yes** (domestic + external) | IMF $3bn + Common Framework relief | 54% | Macro stabilised ~3 yrs; household real income ~4-6 yrs | Ugly entry → **beautiful** resolution [PROVISIONAL] |
| **Argentina (Milei) 2023-25** | Orthodox shock (fiscal anchor) | No (post-2020 restructurings) | IMF + US backstop | 211% y/y (25.5% m/m Dec-23) | Inflation→~2% m/m; poverty 52.9%→31.6% in ~18 mo; GDP +6%+ — but only ~2 yrs in | Trending **beautiful** (early) [SOURCED] |
| **Argentina (pre-2023, decades)** | Heterodox / serial | **Yes** (2001, 2014, 2020) | Intermittent IMF | recurrent | **Lost decades**; serial relapse | **Ugly** |
| **Turkey 2021-24** | Heterodox (rate cuts) → orthodox pivot mid-2023 | No | None (reserves run down) | ~75-85% (2022-23) | Prolonged; inflation still ~50-60% in 2024; real wages lag | **Ugly / prolonged** [SOURCED] |
| **Egypt 2022-24** | Orthodox-ish + IMF, repeated devaluations | No | **$8bn IMF EFF + $35bn UAE Ras El Hekma** windfall | ~34-38% | Stabilising via external cash; structural fragility remains | **Mixed** (windfall-rescued) [SOURCED] |
| **Nigeria 1980s-2005** | SAP, stop-go, serial Paris Club | **Yes** (arrears/reschedulings) | 2005 Paris Club ~$18bn write-off | high/volatile | **~2 lost decades** to debt relief | **Ugly / serial** |

### B.2 Q1 — How often "beautiful" vs "ugly"? (base-rate split)

The honest answer: **truly beautiful inflationary deleveragings — orderly disinflation + falling debt/GDP + household real incomes back to pre-crisis within ~5 years — are the minority.**

- **[ESTIMATED] ~30-40% "beautiful" / ~60-70% "ugly."** Rationale: Reinhart-Rogoff's 100-episode work finds the *mean* time to regain pre-crisis per-capita income is **~8 years** and the *median* ~**6.5 years** [SOURCED] — i.e. the *typical* episode does **not** clear the ~5-year "beautiful" bar on the household-income criterion alone. Layer on the additional "beautiful" requirements (debt/GDP actually falls; no serial relapse/default) and the qualifying fraction falls further. Serial default/relapse is, per Reinhart-Rogoff, "a nearly universal phenomenon" for emerging economies [SOURCED].
- The good tail clusters where the right policy + support combination holds (Ghana, early-Milei Argentina, Egypt-with-windfall); the bad tail clusters in heterodox/under-supported/weak-institution cases (Turkey 2021-23, pre-2023 Argentina, Nigeria 1980s-2005).

### B.3 Q2 — Typical time for real household income/wages to regain pre-crisis level

| Metric | Value | Flag |
|---|---|---|
| Reinhart-Rogoff, 100 banking-crisis episodes — **mean** | **~8 years** to pre-crisis per-capita income | [SOURCED] |
| Reinhart-Rogoff — **median** | **~6.5 years** | [SOURCED] |
| Plausible **range** across the comparator set | **~3 years (best, orthodox + windfall/relief, e.g. Ghana/Argentina-early) to 10-20+ years (serial/lost-decade, e.g. Nigeria 1980s-2005, pre-2023 Argentina)** | [ESTIMATED] |
| **Working median for calibration** | **~6-8 years** for *household real income*; macro/inflation can stabilise in **~2-4 years** | [ESTIMATED] |

Key nuance: **macro stabilisation (inflation, FX, reserves, debt/GDP) runs years ahead of household recovery.** Ghana stabilised the macro in ~3 years while real wages were still climbing out of a ~40%+ hole. Calibrate Nigeria the same way: stable headline ≠ recovered household.

### B.4 Q3 — The 2-3 factors that most separate good from bad outcomes

1. **Orthodox, sustained, fiscally-anchored policy (the single biggest discriminator).** The cases that turned (Ghana, Argentina-Milei, Turkey-after-the-2023-pivot, Egypt) all converged on tight money + a credible fiscal anchor; the lost decades (pre-2023 Argentina, Turkey 2021-23, Nigeria 1980s-2005) ran heterodox/stop-go policy. **Durability matters more than the announcement** — Argentina's *fiscal* surplus, not just rate hikes, is what broke inflation expectations. [SOURCED]
2. **External support / windfall (the accelerant).** An IMF program plus a large exogenous inflow repeatedly converts an ugly entry into an orderly exit: Ghana (IMF + Common Framework debt relief), Egypt (IMF + $35bn UAE Ras El Hekma), Argentina (IMF + US backstop). Cases *without* a buffer (Turkey 2021-23) bled reserves and prolonged the pain. [SOURCED]
3. **Institutions / governance / political durability (the slow-moving determinant of which tail you land in).** Weak governance and reform-reversibility are what produce *serial* relapse (Nigeria's two lost decades; Argentina's repeat defaults). Strong, durable reform commitment is what lets Ghana/early-Milei hold the line.
- **On default specifically:** default is **not** the decisive variable. A *clean, IMF-covered, high-consent* default that resets the debt stock can **speed** recovery (Ghana); *avoiding* default while leaving insolvency unaddressed can **prolong** it (Turkey's heterodox phase). Egypt avoided default *only because* a mega-windfall substituted for restructuring.

### B.5 Q4 — Where does Nigeria sit, and should the 55/25/20 weights shift?

**Nigeria's current episode (June-2023 FX float + unification, orthodox MPR 18.75%→27.5%, NO default, oil/gas windfall, weak governance/security).** [SOURCED]

| Dimension | Nigeria reading | Pulls toward |
|---|---|---|
| Policy regime | Orthodox float + tightening; real rates turning positive; inflation ~34% peak (2024) → ~21% (2025) → projected ~13% (2026) | **Beautiful** [SOURCED] |
| Default | **None** — debt is serviceable, no restructuring needed *but also no mechanical debt/GDP reset* (unlike Ghana) | **Neutral / mildly beautiful** |
| External support / windfall | Oil & gas revenue tailwind; improved fiscal deficit (4.8%→4.1% of GDP); **but no Ghana-style relief and no Egypt-scale FDI windfall** | **Mildly beautiful** |
| Institutions / governance / security | Weak governance, insecurity, history of **reform reversal** (FX/subsidy backsliding pre-2023), thin fiscal anchor vs Argentina's | **Ugly / tail risk** |
| Household reality | Poverty 38.8% (2023) → **47% (2024, ~$2.15/day)**, +14m poor in one year; staples up ~5x 2020-24; **World Bank flags up to 52% by 2026** | **Ugly (household lag is severe)** [SOURCED] |

**Read.** Structurally, Nigeria sits on the **better-than-median side** of the distribution: it has the two ingredients (orthodoxy + a revenue windfall, no debt-solvency crisis) that define the *good* tail of comparators. That genuinely supports a **base case ≈ Ghana/Egypt-style orderly stabilisation** rather than a Turkey/Argentina-serial lost decade.

**But three cross-case facts argue the current 55/25/20 is, if anything, slightly tilted too optimistic:**
1. **The 5-year "household recovery" bar is a <40% event** even for orthodox cases — and that is precisely the *bull* scenario. A 25% bull weight is at or above the empirical base rate for fast household recovery; it is not conservatively set.
2. **Nigeria lacks the two accelerants Ghana/Egypt had** — a mechanical debt reset (Ghana) and a mega-windfall (Egypt). Its windfall is real but moderate, so disinflation will likely be *slower and shallower* than Ghana's spectacular 2025.
3. **Governance/security/reform-reversibility is Nigeria's defining downside** — it is the exact variable that, across the comparator set, sorts countries into the *ugly/serial* tail. The 20% bear weight is thin relative to that structural risk.

**Recommended calibration adjustment.** [PROVISIONAL] Hold the base roughly intact but **shift a few points from bull to bear** — e.g. **55/20/25** (or 50/25/25). The cross-case evidence says: orthodoxy + windfall + no-default earns Nigeria a *credible* base case (not a downgrade of the base), but a fast/"beautiful" household recovery is empirically rarer than 25%, and weak governance fattens the left tail more than 20% allows. Net: **trim bull, fatten bear; keep base.**
