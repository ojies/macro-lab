# Historical Analogues — Turkey (2018–2025) & Egypt (2016–2025): "Managed-Rate → Forced-Devaluation" Crises

**Dataset:** Big-Debt-Cycle / Nigeria — comparative analogues worksheet. Two "managed-FX-rate that breaks under a balance-of-payments squeeze" cases chosen for maximum structural similarity to Nigeria's 2023 float: large current-account/import-cover stress, a defended official rate with a wide parallel premium, an inflation spike on the devaluation, and an IMF/external-anchor question on the back end.
**Compiled:** 2026-06-30.
**Source priority:** IMF (Article IV / program reviews), World Bank, TurkStat/TÜİK & CBRT/TCMB (Turkey), CAPMAS & Central Bank of Egypt (Egypt), with reputable secondary outlets (Bloomberg, Reuters/AP, Carnegie, CFR, Chatham House, World Gold Council) for narrative and dating.

**Flag legend:** `[SOURCED]` = stated by a cited primary/secondary source for the stated period · `[SOURCED-2nd]` = official central-bank/statistics-office figure reported via a secondary outlet · `[ESTIMATED]` = derived/computed by analyst (method noted) · `[PROVISIONAL]` = single-source or vintage-uncertain · blank/`—` = not reliably sourceable, left empty rather than guessed.

**One-line frame:** Turkey ran the *un-orthodox* experiment (cut rates into 85% inflation, 2021–23) and paid for it with a deeper, longer real-wage collapse before a 2023 U-turn; Egypt ran the *orthodox-with-a-sponsor* playbook (IMF programs + Gulf cash + float) and got a faster, cleaner stabilisation. Nigeria's 2023 reform is structurally Egypt's path **without** Egypt's $35bn Gulf cheque — and was executed more like Turkey's *gradualist* tightening in its first year.

| **How it's resolving** | Orthodox since mid-2023; disinflation "widespread" by end-2025 (30.9%); reserves rebuilt; current-account financed. Still **above-target** — a slow, grinding glide, not a clean victory. | [SOURCED] | IMF 2025 Art IV; Şimşek statements |

### A2. Turkey verdict

**UGLY deleveraging (Dalio sense) for ~2021–23, transitioning toward a managed/normalised path post-2023.** The 2021–23 rate-cuts-into-inflation episode is a textbook "monetary debasement to inflate away/avoid the pain" experiment that instead *deepened* the real-wage hole and entrenched dollarization. The mid-2023 reversal began the orthodox cure, but at the cost of two-plus extra years of household pain that an earlier tightening would have shortened. **Household real-income recovery: not yet complete as of end-2025** — real wages remain below 2019–21 levels; on current disinflation (target <20% 2026, single digits 2027), a *full* real-wage recovery realistically lands ~2026–28, i.e. **roughly 5–7 years from the 2021 onset.** [ESTIMATED — synthesis of wage-share and disinflation data]

## PART B — EGYPT (2016 + 2022–2024): the orthodox-with-a-sponsor case

### B1. Snapshot table

| Dimension | Reading | Flag | Source / note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trigger** | 2016: chronic dollar shortage, overvalued managed peg, IMF program precondition. 2022 leg: Russia–Ukraine war spiked the import/commodity bill and triggered a **~$20bn portfolio outflow (Jan–Sep 2022)** off "hot money" carry positions, breaking the defended rate. | [SOURCED] | Crisis Group; Al Jazeera; Cairo Review |
| **Debt currency mix** | Sovereign external debt heavy and rising: **~$164bn external debt (2025)**; debt-service crowds out the budget — **interest ≈ 87% of tax revenue** (2025/26 budget). Large reliance on FX/Eurobond + Gulf deposits + short-term carry inflows. | [SOURCED] | TradingEconomics; EIPR budget analyses |
| **Peak inflation** | **~38% YoY, Sept 2023** (record, urban headline); food +68% YoY mid-2023. | [SOURCED-2nd] | CAPMAS via TradingEconomics/Wikipedia |
| **Inflation path** | 2016 float spike → eased → **2022–23 surge to ~38% (Sep-2023)** → 2024 avg ~28.3% → **2025 avg ~14% (12.3% Nov-2025)** → ~12–13% early-2026. Faster round-trip than Turkey. | [SOURCED-2nd] | CAPMAS via Bloomberg/Xinhua; World Bank MPO |
| **Currency depreciation** | **EGP 8.85/$ (Mar-2016) → ~18 post-2016 float → ~50+/$ (Mar-2024)** after four devaluations (Mar-2022, Oct-2022, Jan-2023 ~40% loss, **Mar-2024 >38% in one step**). Cumulative ~**−82% vs USD 2016→2024**. | [SOURCED] | AP/KSAT; EIPR; Bloomberg |
| **Parallel-market gap** | Pre-float Jan-2024: **official ~31 vs parallel ~72** (gap >100%). The Mar-2024 float **closed the gap**, cleared the FX import backlog, and it has **not reopened** — the single clearest "orthodox worked" signal. | [SOURCED] | Bloomberg; IMF; AGBI |
| **Policy path** | **Orthodox + external anchor.** Successive **IMF programs** ($3bn EFF Dec-2022 → augmented to **$8bn**, Mar-2024). **CBE hiked 600bp to 27.25%** at the Mar-2024 float (peak ~27.25–27.75%). Plus **asset/divestment sales** and the decisive **Ras El-Hekma $35bn UAE deal** (Feb-2024: $24bn land + $11bn deposit conversion) and **~$22bn** combined IMF/EU/World Bank support. 2025: **525bp of rate cuts** as inflation fell. | [SOURCED] | Bloomberg; ING; ADQ; Washington Institute |
| **Reserves / external** | NIR rebuilt post-deal: **official reserves + FX assets ~$67.5bn (Feb-2026)** vs $59.7bn a year earlier; FX backlog cleared, interbank market reopened. | [SOURCED] | CBE via SIS/secondary; IMF |
| **Household outcome** | **National poverty 33.5% (2021/22)**; poverty on the LMIC line **+~5pp 2022→2024**; real spending on every budget line *except debt interest* fell in real terms. Devaluations were regressive (the "3rd flotation shock"). Households hedged into USD and gold during the FX-shortage years. | [SOURCED] | World Bank Poverty & Equity Brief; EIPR |
| **How it's resolving** | **Ras El-Hekma + float + IMF** combination stabilised FX in 2024; pound steady-to-stronger and disinflation through 2025. Chatham House caveat: the rescue was **"more luck (Gulf cash) than skill"** — durability depends on continued reform and not re-accumulating a defended-rate overhang. | [SOURCED] | Chatham House; Capital Economics; MEI |

### B2. Egypt verdict

**Closer to a "BEAUTIFUL-ish" deleveraging — but bought, not earned.** The orthodox package (float + 600bp hike + IMF) genuinely worked on the *mechanics* — parallel gap closed, backlog cleared, inflation halved within ~12 months. But the decisive ingredient was the **$35bn Gulf windfall**, which let Egypt devalue *once, hard, and credibly* instead of bleeding reserves defending a rate. **Household recovery is faster than Turkey's but incomplete:** poverty rose into 2024 and real wages were still depressed, with disinflation only reaching ~12–14% by 2025. A real-income recovery is plausibly **~2025–27, i.e. ~3–4 years from the 2022 onset** — shorter than Turkey because the devaluation was front-loaded and externally cushioned. [ESTIMATED]

## PART D — WHICH DOES NIGERIA RESEMBLE? (and base/bull/bear)

### D1. Structural match

Nigeria's 2023 reform — **June-2023 FX unification/float, "willing-buyer/willing-seller," MPR ratcheted 11.5% (May-22) → 18.75% (Jul-23) → 27.5%, IMF Article IV engagement** — is in its *intent* squarely the **Egypt orthodox-liberalisation template**: kill the multiple-rate regime, let the official rate meet the parallel, tighten to anchor expectations, and lean on the Fund's credibility. It is decisively **NOT** the Turkey heterodox experiment — Nigeria *raised* rates into inflation rather than cutting them. [SOURCED — IMF 2024 Art IV; Trendtype; Businessday]

But two execution differences pull Nigeria's *texture* toward Turkey's slower grind:

1. **Gradualism.** Egypt did a single 600bp shock-hike to 27.25% *simultaneously* with its float; Nigeria tightened **in stages** (18.75% in 2023, only reaching 27.5% later), so inflation stayed stickier and ran hotter for longer through 2024 — the same "too slow" critique levelled at Turkey pre-2023. [SOURCED]
2. **No $35bn cheque.** Egypt's clean stabilisation was *purchased* with the **Ras El-Hekma windfall** that let it devalue once and credibly. **Nigeria has no comparable Gulf/anchor backstop** — it must rebuild reserves the hard way through oil receipts, remittances and portfolio inflows, which lengthens the credibility-building phase and keeps the naira more exposed to renewed slippage (the Turkey risk). [SOURCED — Chatham House on Egypt; IMF on Nigeria]

**Verdict: Nigeria = Egypt's policy *direction* executed at Turkey's *pace*, and without Egypt's external sponsor.** That blend matters: orthodoxy is the right diagnosis, but the *absence of a one-off FX windfall* means Nigeria's parallel-gap closure and disinflation should be **slower and bumpier than Egypt's**, while still avoiding Turkey's self-inflicted detour because the policy stance is genuinely tight.

### D2. Odds for Nigeria (analyst synthesis — [ESTIMATED]/[PROVISIONAL])

| Scenario | What it looks like | Analogue | Rough odds |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BASE** | Orthodoxy held; disinflation gradual; naira range-bound after overshoot; real wages trough then slow climb; **full household real-income recovery ~4–6 years from 2023 (≈2027–29)** | "Egypt's destination, Turkey's timetable" | **~50–55%** |
| **BULL** | Reform stays tight + an external catalyst (sustained oil/gas FDI, large diaspora/portfolio inflows, or a credible IMF-anchored package) substitutes for Egypt's Gulf windfall; parallel gap stays closed; recovery compresses toward **~3–4 years** | Egypt 2024–25 | **~20–25%** |
| **BEAR** | Tightening reversed prematurely / fiscal (CBN financing) slippage / oil shock; naira re-slides, inflation re-accelerates, dollarization entrenches; recovery stretches **>7 years** | Turkey 2021–22 relapse | **~25–30%** |

Odds are an analyst's qualitative synthesis of the two analogues against Nigeria's known constraints, **not** a formal model. [ESTIMATED/PROVISIONAL]

### D3. Bottom line

Nigeria chose the *right* (Egypt) door, not the Turkish one — it is tightening, not debasing. But because it is doing so **gradually and without a $35bn sponsor**, the most likely outcome is a *real* but *slow* stabilisation: think **Egypt's mechanics on a multi-year delay**, with the tail risk that fiscal or political pressure triggers a Turkey-style premature loosening before disinflation is locked in. **Household real incomes most plausibly bottom 2024–25 and do not fully recover until ~2027–29** in the base case. [ESTIMATED]
