**Compiled:** 2026-06-30 · **Analyst pass:** primary/official sources prioritized (NBS, CBN, World Bank, IMF, OPEC/NUPRC)

> **READ FIRST — two structural breaks dominate this module.** Nigeria's statistical agency rebased **two** of the series here in 2025:
> 1. **CPI rebased (Jan 2025 data, published 18 Feb 2025):** base year 2009→2024, basket reweighted. Headline y/y fell mechanically from **34.80% (Dec 2024, old series)** to **24.48% (Jan 2025, new series)** — ~10 points of the drop is methodological, not disinflation.
> 2. **GDP rebased (published 21 Jul 2025):** base year 2010→2019. Nominal 2024 GDP revised up **+34.4%** (₦277.49 trn → ₦372.82 trn), which **mechanically lowered every ratio with GDP in the denominator** (debt/GDP, deficit/GDP).

**The real collapse (key finding):** real minimum wage fell from **100 (2015) to a trough of 50.5 (2023)** — roughly **halving** — as ₦30,000 was held fixed through 24%+ inflation. Two caveats: (i) 2019 and 2024 are blended years (raise took effect Apr 2019 / May 2024); (ii) 2023's ~$47 uses the full-year average and masks the intra-year collapse from ~$65 (H1, ₦460) to ~$38 (H2, ₦800) after the June-2023 float.

## 6. REBASING RECONCILIATION (the core deliverable)

Two NBS rebasings in 2025 break the time series. Each must be bridged before pre-2025 and post-2025 figures are compared, otherwise the dataset will read a methodological step-change as real economic improvement.

### 6.1 CPI rebasing — January 2025 (published 18 Feb 2025)

**The break, in one line:** headline y/y went **34.80% (Dec 2024, old 2009=100) → 24.48% (Jan 2025, new 2024=100)** — a ~10-point drop that is **mostly mechanical** (new base year + lower food weight), not disinflation.

| Metric | Old (2009=100), Dec 2024 | New (2024=100), Jan 2025 | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headline y/y | **34.80%** | **24.48%** | [SOURCED] |
| Food y/y | **39.84%** | **26.08%** | [SOURCED] |
| Core y/y | — | 22.59% | [SOURCED] |
| Urban y/y | — | 26.09% | [SOURCED] |
| Rural y/y | — | 22.15% | [SOURCED] |

(New-series index: Jan 2025 = 110.68, with Dec 2024 = 100.00, Jan 2024 = 88.9.)

**What changed** (NBS "Highlights of CPI Rebasing", Feb 2025):
- **Price-reference (base) period:** 2009 → **2024**; **weight-reference period:** **2023**.
- **Basket:** 740 → **934 product varieties** (media "960" is erroneous; official is 934).
- **Classification:** COICOP 1999 (12 divisions) → **COICOP 2018 (13 divisions)**.
- **Weight data:** 2023 Nigeria Living Standards Survey + 2024 Survey of Rare Items; admin data (CBN, NAICOM, National Accounts/Trade).
- **Collection:** paper → **digital CAPI**. **Formulas:** Dutot → **Jevons** (elementary); Lowe → **Young / Modified Laspeyres** (weighted).
- **Reweighting (2009 → 2024):** Food & Non-Alcoholic Beverages **51.8% → 40.0%**; Housing/Utilities 16.7% → 8.4%; **Restaurants & Accommodation 1.2% → 12.9%** (meals-away-from-home moved out of Food); Transport 6.5% → 10.7%; Health 3.0% → 6.1%; Education 3.9% → 6.2%; Info & Comms 0.7% → 3.3%.

**The lower food weight + new base together explain most of the headline drop.**

**Implication for the dataset:**
- **Inflation *levels*** are not comparable across Dec 2024 / Jan 2025. Do not read "34.8% → 24.5%" as 10 points of disinflation.
- **Inflation *rates* can still be chained** to build a continuous deflator (a y/y rate measures price change regardless of base). The real-minimum-wage index in §3.2 does exactly this — it is methodologically valid across the break, with the caveat that 2025–2026 inputs are estimates.
- **A second, smaller break occurred in late 2025 — see §6.2.**

### 6.2 Second CPI methodology revision — late 2025

NBS switched the y/y reference from a **single-month base** to a **twelve-month-average index reference (avg 2024 = 100)**, which **retroactively revised Nov 2025 up from 14.45% to 17.33%**; Dec 2025 onward (15.15%, …) use the revised method. So **Jan–Nov 2025 (as originally published)** and **Dec 2025 → 2026** sit on slightly different y/y computations. The dataset's 2025 headline value of 17.33 corresponds to this revised Nov-2025 figure; treat Jan–Nov 2025 originals and Dec 2025+ as a minor sub-break.

**The break, in one line:** base year **2010 → 2019**; nominal 2024 GDP revised up **+34.4%** (₦277.49 trn → ₦372.82 trn), better capturing the **digital economy, fintech, creative industries, real estate, and the informal sector** (real estate rose to 3rd-largest subsector; crude oil fell to 5th).

Despite the +30–40% revision, Nigeria **remained the 4th-largest African economy** (behind South Africa, Egypt, Algeria) — the ranking did **not** change (unlike the 2014 rebasing).

**Mechanical effect on ratios (numerator unchanged, denominator larger):**

| Ratio | Before rebasing | After rebasing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Debt/GDP, **Q4 2024** | **52.13%** (₦144.67 trn / ₦277.49 trn) | **38.80%** (same debt / ₦372.82 trn) | DMO |
| Debt/GDP, **Q1 2025** | — | **39.4%** (₦149.39 trn / ₦379.17 trn) — below FG's 40% self-limit | DMO |
| Domestic-debt share, Q4 2024 | 26.80% | 19.95% | DMO |
| External-debt share, Q4 2024 | 25.33% | 18.85% | DMO |

> **Critical caveat:** the ~13-point fall in debt/GDP (52.1% → 38.8%) is **purely a denominator effect**. Total public debt kept **rising** in nominal terms (₦144.67 trn Q4-2024 → ₦149.39 trn Q1-2025 → ₦159.27 trn by Q4-2025). This is **not deleveraging.** The same mechanic flatters **deficit/GDP**.

**Implication for the dataset's affected ratios:**
- `debt_to_gdp_imf_artIV_pct` and any deficit/GDP, debt-service/GDP, or GDP-share metric: pre-2025 values sit on the **2010-base** GDP; 2025+ IMF/DMO values sit on the **2019-base** GDP. A pre-2025 "52.9%" and a 2025 "52.0%" in the gauges CSV are **not directly comparable** — the post-rebasing denominator is ~34% larger. To compare like-for-like, either (a) restate pre-2025 debt/GDP on the rebased GDP (which would lower them ~25%), or (b) restate post-2025 on the old base (raise them ~34%).
- **Ratios with GDP in the *numerator or as a level* (e.g. GDP/capita USD, GDP USD bn)** also jump at the break — the IMF WEO CSV's per-capita and USD-GDP figures already reflect this where they post-date the rebasing.
- **Ratios *not* involving GDP** (debt-service-to-revenue, reserves in USD, FX rate, oil mbpd) are **unaffected** by the GDP rebasing.

**Prior context — 2014 GDP rebasing:** base 1990 → 2010, lifting nominal GDP ~**59.7%** and making Nigeria Africa's largest economy at the time. The 2025 uplift (~41.7% headline / 34.4% for 2024) was smaller and did not change the ranking.

### 6.4 Quick "do / don't" for time-series users

| Series | Safe to compare across 2024/2025? | How to bridge |
|---|---|---|
| CPI **level** y/y | **No** (Jan-2025 CPI rebasing) | Use chained y/y *rates*, not levels |
| Food inflation level | **No** | Same |
| Real-wage / deflator (chained) | **Yes** | Built from rates — already bridges the break |
| Debt/GDP, deficit/GDP, GDP-share | **No** (2025 GDP rebasing, +34%) | Restate one side onto the same base year |
| GDP/capita USD, GDP USD bn | **No** | Note +34% level shift in 2025 |
| Debt-service/revenue, reserves USD, FX, oil mbpd | **Yes** | No rebasing touches these |
