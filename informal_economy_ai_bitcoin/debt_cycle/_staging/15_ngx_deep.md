## 6. Structural milestones

- **2021 — Demutualization completed.** The member-owned, non-profit NSE converted into a profit-oriented public holding company, **Nigerian Exchange Group Plc (NGX Group)**, with three subsidiaries: **Nigerian Exchange Limited (NGX)** — the operating exchange; **NGX Regulation Ltd (NGX RegCo)** — independent regulation; and **NGX Real Estate Ltd (NGX RelCo)**. `[SOURCED]`
- **October 2021 — Self-listing.** NGX Group Plc listed its own shares on the Exchange under ticker **NGXGROUP** (listing by introduction). `[SOURCED]` (exact date `[GAP: precise day not sourced]`, commonly cited mid-Oct-2021).
- **14-April-2022 — Derivatives market launch.** NGX launched its **Exchange-Traded Derivatives (ETD)** market with two equity index-futures contracts: **NGX 30 Index Futures** and **NGX Pension Index Futures**. `[SOURCED]`
- **ETFs & instruments:** Post-demutualization the group hosts **>393 listed securities**, comprising **~151 companies, ~157 fixed-income instruments, 12 ETFs, and 4 index futures**. `[SOURCED]`
- **Fixed income / FMDQ relationship:** NGX lists corporate/sovereign bonds and sukuk, but the **bulk of FGN bonds, T-bills, OTC FX and money-market trading clears on FMDQ Exchange**, not NGX — i.e. Nigeria's debt-capital and FX market depth sits largely outside the NGX equities board. `[Contextual — light sourcing; treat as background]`
- **Bank recapitalisation (2024–2026):** CBN's 2024 minimum-capital directive triggered a wave of bank rights issues/public offers that added fresh equity capital and boosted both market cap and turnover (NGX cited recap + new listings adding multiple trillions of naira to market cap). `[SOURCED basis]`

- Intraday at unification (13-Jun-2023) USD market cap fell ~36% (≈$65.5bn → ≈$41.9bn) in days. `[SOURCED]`

### "Equities as inflation hedge" analysis

The NGX ASI printed **successive all-time highs in naira terms** through the devaluation cycle — +45.9% (2023), +37.65% (2024), +51.19% (2025) — a cumulative naira gain of roughly **+200% over 2023–2025** and the best three-year run in a generation (2025 was the strongest single year in 18 years). Against domestic inflation that ran ~25–35% annually over 2023–2025, equities delivered **positive real naira returns in 2023 and especially 2025**, and roughly **flat-to-modestly-positive real returns in 2024** — confirming that Nigerian large-cap equities (cement, telecoms, consumer staples, banks with FX-revaluation gains) acted as a **partial hedge against inflation and naira debasement**. Pricing power in hard-asset/dollar-linked businesses (Dangote/BUA cement, Seplat/Aradel oil, MTN/Airtel) is exactly what one expects to outrun local CPI.

But the **dollar lens tells the debt-cycle story**: USD market cap **collapsed from ~$65bn (mid-2023) to ~$38–41bn (2024)** as the float repriced the naira ~3x weaker, and only **recovered to ~$68.7bn by end-2025** once the naira stabilised/firmed. In other words, **it took ~2.5 years of record-breaking nominal gains for the market merely to claw back, in dollars, to its pre-devaluation 2023 level** — the entire spectacular naira bull run largely *offset the currency loss* rather than creating new dollar wealth. This is the classic big-debt-cycle signature: in a currency-debasement/inflationary deleveraging, **nominal asset prices soar while real (and especially hard-currency) values stagnate or fall**, so headline naira records mask the dollar-wealth destruction borne by anyone measuring in USD or in imported goods. The equity market hedged inflation for naira-based holders far better than cash or bonds, but it did **not** preserve dollar wealth until the currency itself stabilised in 2025.

**Conflict / caveat:** end-2024 USD market cap is shown as `[ESTIMATED]` ~$40.9bn (N62.76tn ÷ ~N1,535/$); a third-party data series cited ~$54.4bn for 2024 (likely a different reference date or rate, or a total-securities rather than equities-only basis). The end-2023 (~$44.9bn) and end-2025 ($68.74bn, sourced) anchors plus the float-window points (Jun-2023 $65.5bn → Jun-2024 $37.7bn) are the robust spine of the dollar series. NGX also reports a wider "total market capitalisation" (equities + bonds + ETFs) materially larger than the equities-only cap used here — keep bases separate.
