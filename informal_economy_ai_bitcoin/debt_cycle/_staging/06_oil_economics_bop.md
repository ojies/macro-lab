## CRITICAL METHODOLOGY NOTES (read first)

**A. The 2023 subsidy-removal break is NOT clean.** President Tinubu declared "subsidy is gone" at inauguration on **29 May 2023** (PMS pump price tripled from ~₦185 to ~₦540+/litre in June 2023). But a **de-facto / implicit petrol subsidy re-emerged through H2-2023 and all of 2024** because (i) the naira float (Jun 2023) and Feb-2024 devaluation pushed the landing cost above the capped pump price, and (ii) NNPCL remained sole PMS importer selling below cost. The IMF, World Bank and NNPCL itself documented this. **Full de-facto deregulation only occurred in October 2024**, once the Dangote refinery began supplying and NNPC stopped subsidising the Dangote off-take. So treat **mid-2023 → Sep-2024 as a transition, NOT a hard stop.**

**B. Post-float NGN revenue inflation.** From mid-2023 the naira moved from ~₦460/$ to ~₦1,500–1,650/$ (see worksheet 03). Any oil-revenue or federation-revenue figure expressed in **NGN trillion roughly tripled in nominal terms after the float without a comparable USD increase** — e.g. federation revenue ₦16.8tn (2023) → ₦31.9tn (2024) is largely a naira-valuation effect, not real-dollar growth. Always read NGN revenue series alongside the FX break.

**C. BoP vintages / revisions.** CBN re-based and revised the BoP in 2025–2026. The **April-2025 release** reported 2024 current+capital account = $17.22bn and net errors & omissions (NEO) = –$5.10bn; the **March-2026 release** revised 2024 current account up to **$19.03bn** and 2024 NEO to **–$9.38bn**. Both vintages are shown where they differ. World Bank/IMF IFS current-account values (used as the 2015–2022 backbone) are a different vintage again.

**D. Bonny Light price source.** Annual averages below are OPEC's official **Bonny Light spot price** from the OPEC Annual Statistical Bulletin (ASB) — the cleanest free primary series. EIA's "U.S. Landed Cost of Bonny Light" is withheld ("W") in recent years and unusable.

| 2024 | ~7.8 to 8.43 (estimates) | **Implicit subsidy RE-EMERGED** (naira weakness); NNPC owed ₦7.8tn for 7 mths to Jul-24; IMF est ₦8.43tn full-yr (~half of projected ₦17.7tn oil revenue) | NNPC CFO / IMF [PROVISIONAL — competing estimates] |
| 2025 | ≈0 (deregulated) | **Full de-facto deregulation from Oct-2024**; Dangote–NNPC competition set market PMS prices; no budgeted subsidy | CBN/World Bank/secondary [SOURCED — narrative] |

**Subsidy-break documentation (REQUIRED FLAG):**
- **29 May 2023:** Tinubu announces "subsidy is gone"; pump price ~₦185 → ~₦540+/litre in June 2023.
- **H2-2023 → Sep-2024:** **Re-emergence as implicit subsidy.** The naira float (Jun-23) and Feb-24 devaluation raised PMS landing cost above the capped pump price. IMF (Mar-2024) said Nigeria had "reinstated" fuel subsidies; NNPCL (Aug-2024) confirmed ₦7.8tn ($4.9bn) owed by FG for 2024.
- **October 2024:** "Finally, subsidy is gone" — NNPC sold PMS at ₦1,030/litre (Abuja); full deregulation as Dangote refinery supply ramped.
- **2025:** Market-priced PMS; prices fell from ~₦1,030 toward ~₦860/litre on Dangote–NNPC competition (Feb-25). PMS prices up ~643% vs pre-removal.

**Quarterly BoP (recent, USD bn):**
- Q3-2024 current account surplus: **+6.1** (CBN/Proshare) [SOURCED]
- Q1-2025 current account surplus: **+3.73** (CBN) [SOURCED]; Q1-2025 FDI –19.35% to $250m [SOURCED]
- Q3-2025 overall BoP surplus: **+4.60**; current account surplus –41% y/y [SOURCED]

**BoP narrative (debt-cycle relevant):** Nigeria swung from chronic current-account/BoP deficits (2019–2023 reserve drawdown) to a **2024 BoP surplus of $6.83bn** — the turnaround driven by import compression (petroleum imports –23.2%), gas exports (+48.3%), resurgent **portfolio inflows ($13.35bn, +106.5%)** after the naira float, and resilient remittances ($20.93bn). FDI stayed weak ($1.08bn, –42.3%) — inflows are hot-money/portfolio, not sticky FDI. In **2025** the surplus narrowed to $4.23bn as portfolio inflows halved (–48.3%), the primary-income deficit widened (+60.9%, dividend/interest outflows), and crude exports fell –14.4%.
