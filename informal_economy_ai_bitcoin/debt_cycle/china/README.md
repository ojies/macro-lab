## What the model finds
- **The debt ramp:** total non-financial debt **127% → 300% of GDP** (2000–2025) while growth slid 14% → ~5%. The **credit-intensity of growth tripled** — each point of GDP growth now takes **~3.1× the credit** it did in the 2000s (0.41 → 1.30 debt-pts per growth-pt). Diminishing returns to the debt-fuelled-investment engine.
- **The property bust + deflation:** real-estate investment **~14% → ~6% of GDP** (more than halved since 2015), with **CPI near zero (0.1–0.2%) in 2023–25** — balance-sheet-recession / deflation risk.
- **The demographic cliff — "old before rich":** working-age share peaked 72.9% (2010) → 69.7%; fertility **1.69 → 1.0** (among the world's lowest); population shrinking since ~2022. China is aging at **~⅓ of Japan's 1990 income** (~$13.7k GNI/capita, right at the high-income line).
- **Diagnosis:** growth-model *exhaustion*, not (yet) a crisis. The Japan "balance-sheet recession" setup — but at middle income with the high-income transition unresolved. China's **closed capital account + state banks + ~$3.2tn reserves** let it *force* a slow, forbearing workout — avoiding a market crash but risking a decade of near-stagnation.

## Where China sits among the poles
Not Nigeria's FX crisis (China is a net external creditor), not the US's reserve privilege (no open capital account), not the euro area's union-without-fiscal-union bind (China has full monetary-fiscal sovereignty) — a **managed, Japan-style grind**: vast administrative policy space, but a structural trilemma no lever fully resolves. The development-age model's escaper now meets its own debt cycle — **the catch-up and the debt cycle are two chapters of one story.**
