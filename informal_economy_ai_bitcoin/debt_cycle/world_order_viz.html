macro-lab · capstone synthesis

Where the world is going

The incumbents are all late in the same debt cycle at once. The money is fragmenting. The people are moving south.

Drawn from the lab's 17 models across five economies. The through-line: the debt ratio is not the danger — policy space is, and the deepest current underneath it all is demographic. This is a lens, not a prophecy.

01 · the deepest current

The people are moving south

Every economy below its replacement line (2.1 births/woman) is set to shrink once age-momentum fades. Almost the entire developed and risen world is there — South Korea at 0.72, China at 1.00, Japan 1.20 — while the labour force of the century sits in the young South. Bubble size = population; only Nigeria and Indonesia are still above replacement.

Total fertility rate (2023, World Bank) vs median age. The vertical line is replacement (2.1). Left of it, births no longer replace deaths — a shrinking population is only a matter of time. South Korea's 0.72 is the lowest ever recorded. (A full birth-vs-death / population-to-2100 model is being added to the lab.)

02 · the money

The reserve order is fragmenting — slowly

Not dollar collapse (nothing can yet replace it) but a steady erosion of the unipolar system: the dollar's share of global reserves has slid from 72% to 57% since 2000, and foreign ownership of US Treasuries is thinning. Central banks are buying gold; capital is drifting toward assets that sit on no one's balance sheet.

USD share of allocated global FX reserves (IMF COFER) and foreign-held share of US Treasuries (Treasury TIC). Source: macro-lab usa_debt_cycle.py.

03 · the cycle

A synchronized late-cycle world

The incumbents are late in their big debt cycles together — and the lab's counter-intuitive finding is that the debt ratio doesn't rank the danger. Nigeria carries the least and the most acute risk; Japan the most and no crisis.

Government debt, % of GDP (2025). The order of risk is nearly the reverse — set by policy space: currency sovereignty, who owns the debt, reserve status. Source: compare_poles.py.

04 · the alignment

What the world is aligning with

①
Neutral & real assets
Gold, Bitcoin, commodities, land — what no state can print or freeze, as the reserve issuer's own resolution becomes inflation, not default.
②
Sovereignty as the premium
Who controls their money, energy, food, chips. The dividing line — not rich/poor. The euro periphery and most EMs lack it.
③
The young South as the frontier
The labour and consumers the aging North lost — if it converts the dividend. The lab warns Nigeria's is "structurally unearned."
④
Digital dollar rails
For the dollar-scarce: stablecoins are dollar access without a US bank; AI-underwritten credit is institutions without a strong state.

05 · how it can go

Three roads

Ordered multipolarityThe dollar stays first-among-several; gold/BTC and regional currencies take share; the young South industrializes selectively. Slower, more balanced.
Messy fragmentationTrade/tech blocs harden, a euro-periphery or China-property workout breaks badly, the South's dividend is squandered. Volatile, lower-growth.
A new synthesisDigital rails + AI productivity + African demography combine into a genuine new growth engine. The case the lab's application layer is built for.
The models don't pick one — they tell you what to watch: the USD reserve share · the euro Bund spread · China's credit-intensity & property · the BoJ's exit · and the swing factor in every Nigeria model — governance.
