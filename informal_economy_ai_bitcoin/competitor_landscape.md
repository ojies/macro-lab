# Competitor & Landscape Teardown: Serving Africa's Informal Economy

*Market research briefing — prepared 29 June 2026. Focus: Nigeria-first, pan-African. All figures fresh-sourced (2024–2026) with inline citations. A standing caveat applies throughout: naira (NGN), Kenyan shilling (KES) and Egyptian pound (EGP) devaluation makes USD-reported growth volatile — dollar figures frequently understate local-currency momentum.*

---

## Executive Summary

Africa's informal economy — roughly 80–90% of employment and the overwhelming majority of retail transactions — is being contested across three distinct layers: **payment rails/agent banking**, **B2B e-commerce & embedded credit**, and **crypto/stablecoin cross-border**. The verdict from this teardown: **Layer 1 (payments) is saturated and consolidating into an oligopoly; Layer 2 (B2B retail) has structurally failed on pure-distribution economics and is mid-pivot to fintech; Layer 3 (stablecoin/crypto) is the genuine open frontier**, but it is bifurcating fast into a regulated-infrastructure tier (well-capitalised) and a long tail of thinly-funded freelancer-payout apps. The white space sits at the intersection: embedded, AI-assisted, stablecoin-settled working capital delivered through the agent/POS distribution that Layer 1 already owns but under-monetises.

---

## Layer 1 — Payment Rails / Agent Banking / POS

This is the most mature and most crowded layer. Nigeria's "big three" — **OPay, Moniepoint, PalmPay** — captured the informal market during the 2022–23 naira-redesign cash crunch and chronic legacy-bank outages, and three of them are now profitable. The land grab is essentially over; competition has shifted to monetisation depth, lending, and diaspora corridors.

| Player | Scale / Traction (latest) | Model | Funding / Valuation | Profitable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Moniepoint** | 1M+ POS terminals; 6M businesses; 16M customers; ₦412tn (~$294B) FY25 value; claims ~80% of in-person payments | Acquiring/gateway fees, loan interest, interchange | Series C $200M total (2024–25) at >$1B | Scale-focused; >$100M ARR |
| **OPay** | ~50M registered; 36M MAU; 563k agents (~37% of Nigeria's); ~$12B/mo | Transfer/agent fees, float, lending | $400M (2021) at $2B; **preparing US IPO ~$4B** | Yes (since May 2024) |
| **PalmPay** | 35M+ registered; 1M+ agents across all 774 LGAs; ~$6B/mo | Fees, lending, device financing, FX | ~$140M raised; Series B (~$50–100M) in talks | Yes ($64M rev 2023) |
| **Paga** | ~21–23M users; ₦17.1tn ($12B) in 2025 (+96%); Paga Engine powers 200+ clients | Take rates, infra fees (Engine), SMB software | ~$45M raised; sub-unicorn | Yes (since 2024) |
| **Kuda** | 7M+ customers; ₦14.3tn ($9.3B) Q1'25; app-only neobank | Loan interest, treasury, fees | $55M (2021) at $500M (flat 4 yrs) | Losses cut ~84%; near-breakeven |
| **M-Pesa** (Safaricom) | >50M customers, >500k agents across 8 markets; KES 38.3tn value; ~$7B/mo business volume | Tiered fees, lending share (Fuliza/M-Shwari) | Safaricom ~€7.7B mkt cap; Vodacom raising stake to 55% | Yes (core profit engine) |
| **Wave** | 20M+ MAU; 8+ francophone markets; flat 1% P2P fee | 1% P2P + merchant/bill fees | $200M (2021) at **$1.7B** (stale); +$137M debt 2025 | YC top-50 earner |

**Strengths of the layer:** dense agent/POS distribution (a genuine, hard-to-replicate physical moat), profitability achieved, and blue-chip strategic backing (Visa, Mastercard, Google, IFC, SoftBank). **Weaknesses/gaps:** heavy Nigeria/single-market concentration and FX exposure; intensifying regulatory pressure (the April 2024 CBN onboarding freeze hit OPay/PalmPay/Kuda/Moniepoint; EFCC AML warnings to OPay in Dec 2025; fraud losses hit ₦52bn sector-wide in 2024); and thin revenue disclosure. Crucially, **these players own distribution to the informal retailer but monetise it shallowly** — mostly via transaction fees and consumer micro-loans, not deep working-capital or supply-chain credit.

**Verdict: SATURATED.** A new payments-only entrant faces three profitable, deep-pocketed incumbents with physical agent moats. Entry here is value-destructive unless via an unserved corridor or a differentiated rail.

## Layer 2 — B2B E-Commerce & Embedded Credit for Informal Retailers

This is the layer that **broke**. The 2020–22 thesis — digitise the dukas/kiosks via FMCG marketplaces and lend against transaction data — collided with structurally thin (~9% gross) distribution margins. The result through 2023–26 has been down-rounds, mass layoffs, founder exits, one major cross-border merger, and an outright sector-wide pivot toward fintech/embedded credit (the only margin pool that works).

| Player | Status | Scale | Funding / Valuation | Health |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **OmniRetail** (Omnibiz) | Asset-light; the survivor | 150k+ retailers; ₦1.3tn ($810M) processed 2024; OmniPay ~$12M/mo, <0.5% NPL | $20M Series A (Apr 2025), Norfund + Timon; ~$38M total | **Profitable** (net, 2024); FT #1 fastest-growing 2025 & 2026 |
| **TradeDepot** (ShopTopUp) | Vertically integrated; quiet | "100k+ retailers" (stale 2021 figure); ~$27.6M est. rev 2024 | $110M (2021), no equity since (~4.5 yrs) | Stable but opaque; headcount declining |
| **Sabi** | **Pivoted out of retail** | Peak 300k merchants / $1B GMV; now ~20k tons/mo minerals | $38M Series B (2023) at $300M; none since | Pivoted to commodity-export traceability (TRACE); ~20% layoffs Jun 2025 |
| **MaxAB-Wasoko** | Merged, fintech-first | 450k+ merchants (5 markets); Egypt fintech rev >$180M, >99% repayment | **Down-round**: $625M (2022) → $526M → ~$293M; no new capital since 2022 | Restructuring; burn cut $2M→$500k/mo; founder exits |
| **Twiga Foods** | **Active distress** | Peak 140k outlets; headcount 1,000+ → ~435 (→ skeleton "NewCo") | ~$150M+ raised; $35M emergency convertible (2023) ceded control | Investor (Juven/Creadev) control; liquidation petitions; suspended Nairobi ops Jun 2025 |

**The pattern is unambiguous:** GMV-maximisation is dead; net contribution margin and embedded credit are the new narrative. Only **OmniRetail** has cracked profitability — by staying asset-light, keeping NPLs under 0.5%, and acquiring a POS player (Traction Apps, Oct 2024) to deepen underwriting data. Everyone else either pivoted (Sabi → minerals), merged defensively into a down-round (Wasoko + MaxAB), or is being wound down under investor control (Twiga). The capital winter is real: outside OmniRetail's $20M, no significant primary equity has entered this sector since 2022–23.

**Strengths of survivors:** proprietary transaction data for underwriting; manufacturer relationships; distribution to hundreds of thousands of informal retailers. **Weaknesses/gaps:** structurally thin margins; opaque/stale public metrics; loan-book quality is the make-or-break variable and is mostly undisclosed; heavy single-market FX concentration.

**Verdict: SATURATED BY FAILURE.** The pure-play B2B marketplace model is largely discredited. The surviving opportunity is narrow — embedded credit done with OmniRetail-grade discipline — and the incumbents who survived already occupy it. A new entrant should treat this layer as a *capability to embed*, not a business to rebuild.

## Layer 3 — Crypto / Stablecoin / Cross-Border

This is the **open frontier**. Macro tailwinds are strong: stablecoins are ~43% of Sub-Saharan Africa's crypto volume (Chainalysis 2025); SSA received $205B+ on-chain July 2024–June 2025 (+52% YoY); and stablecoin B2B payments globally jumped from <$100M/month (early 2023) to >$6B by mid-2025 (BCG). The layer is bifurcating into a **well-capitalised regulated-infrastructure tier** and a **long tail of thin-funded freelancer-payout apps**.

| Player | Role | Scale / Traction | Funding | Rail |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Yellow Card** | Largest licensed on/off-ramp; B2B API | $3B+ cumulative; 20 countries; ~30k businesses; rev ~7x since 2023 | $33M Series C (2024); ~$85M total; ~$200M+ val | Stablecoin (USDT/USDC/PYUSD) |
| **Bitnob** | BTC-Lightning + stablecoin B2B infra | $4.5B+ processed; ~30 corridors; powers Strike in Africa | Opaque (Seed; Tether on cap table) | **Lightning + stablecoin** |
| **Noah** | B2B stablecoin rails (SWIFT alt) | $1B+ volume; 70+ countries | $22M Seed (2025) | Stablecoin |
| **TransFi** | Global stablecoin orchestration | 2M+ users; 16x rev growth; ~$5B FY26 target | $19.2M (2026) | Stablecoin |
| **Hurupay** | Stablecoin USD accounts for freelancers | $50M+ processed; $10M+ Feb'26; profitable | $150k pre-seed | Stablecoin (Base) |
| **Payd** | Multi-currency accounts / "OS for borderless work" | $1.6M/mo; ~14k users; break-even | ~$80k grants | Stablecoin (10 chains) |
| **Yogupay** | SME cross-border (Africa–China) | $500M+ cumulative (self-reported); 200+ businesses | Undisclosed/bootstrapped | Mostly fiat + stablecoin |
| **Busha** | Regulated retail exchange | 800k+ users | $4.2M (2021) | Crypto/stablecoin |
| **Quidax** | First SEC-licensed exchange → B2B infra | 1M+ customers; pivoting to API/liquidity | $120k disclosed | Crypto/stablecoin |
| **Strike "Send Globally"** | Lightning remittance (US→Africa via Bitnob) | Strike total $6B+ (2024), profitable, $0 CAC | $80M (2022) at ~$300M; Tether merger pending | **Lightning** |

**Key structural observations.** First, **stablecoins (USDC/USDT) are the dominant rail** — every player except Strike is stablecoin-native. **Bitcoin Lightning is used by only two: Bitnob and Strike** (a single distribution partnership). If a thesis hinges on Lightning specifically, the competitive field is nearly empty — but so is proven demand outside the Strike–Bitnob corridor. Second, **B2B/infrastructure is where capital and margin concentrate**: Yellow Card, Bitnob, Noah, TransFi and Quidax have all pivoted from consumer to B2B rails, leaving the consumer remittance/freelancer niche to under-funded players (Hurupay, Payd — both sub-$200k). Third, **regulatory legitimacy is becoming the moat**: Yellow Card holds the continent's deepest license portfolio (20 countries, now Swiss AML approval) plus Visa/Mastercard/PayPal tie-ins; Busha and Quidax won Nigeria's first SEC provisional licenses (2024).

**Strengths of the layer:** explosive volume growth, high margins (stablecoin take-rates of 0.15–2% with low capital intensity), and a real, growing remittance/B2B-settlement pain point. **Weaknesses/gaps:** almost universally self-reported/unaudited metrics; regulatory uncertainty (Nigeria's posture toward crypto remains volatile); thin capitalisation across the long tail; and a crowded, undifferentiated freelancer-payout micro-segment. Critically, **almost no one connects stablecoin rails to the physical informal retailer** — these are diaspora/freelancer/SME-treasury products, not duka/kiosk products.

**Verdict: OPEN.** Volumes are growing 50%+ YoY, no single player owns informal-economy distribution, and the incumbents (Yellow Card, Bitnob) are infrastructure plays that *enable* rather than *occupy* the last mile.

## Synthesis: Saturated vs. Open, and the White Space

```
Saturation ◄──────────────────────────────────────────► Open
Layer 1 (Payments)   Layer 2 (B2B retail)        Layer 3 (Stablecoin)
[OLIGOPOLY]          [FAILED → fintech pivot]    [FRONTIER, fragmenting]
3 profitable         1 profitable survivor       2 leaders + long tail
incumbents,          (OmniRetail), rest in       of thin-funded apps;
physical agent       distress/down-rounds        Lightning nearly empty
moats
```

**Where the layers connect — and where no one sits.** Each layer solves one problem and ignores the adjacent one:

- **Layer 1** owns *distribution* (agents, POS, the informal retailer's daily cash flow) but monetises it shallowly — transaction fees and consumer micro-loans, not supply-chain working capital.
- **Layer 2** owns *retailer credit & inventory data* but lacks profitable distribution economics and is retreating into embedded fintech.
- **Layer 3** owns *cheap, instant, dollar-denominated rails* but is aimed at diaspora and SMEs, not the duka on the corner.

**The white space is the intersection: AI-underwritten, stablecoin-settled working capital delivered through existing agent/POS distribution to informal retailers and cross-border informal traders.** Concretely, the unoccupied positions are:

1. **Dollar-stable store-of-value + credit for the informal retailer.** Naira/cedi/shilling devaluation is the informal merchant's single biggest wealth destroyer. No one offers an informal retailer a stablecoin-denominated balance *and* inventory credit through the POS they already use. Layer 1 has the distribution but not the rail; Layer 3 has the rail but not the merchant relationship.

2. **Cross-border informal trade settlement.** Yogupay gestures at the Africa–China corridor for SMEs, but the millions of *informal* cross-border traders (Lagos–Cotonou–Guangzhou) remain on cash/hawala. A stablecoin product priced and distributed for them is open.

3. **AI-native underwriting as the wedge.** OmniRetail proved sub-0.5% NPLs are achievable with good data discipline; the rest of Layer 2 could not. An AI-first underwriting layer that plugs into Layer 1's transaction data and disburses in stablecoin would attack the exact failure point that sank Twiga, Sabi's retail arm, and Wasoko.

4. **Lightning's near-empty field.** Only Bitnob and Strike use Lightning, and only in one partnership. The competitive field is thin — but a new entrant must validate demand that, so far, only Strike–Bitnob has proven. This is white space *and* unproven space; treat it as an option, not the core.

### Competitive-Positioning Matrix

| | **Owns informal distribution** | **Owns stablecoin/USD rail** | **Owns retailer credit data** | **Profitable** |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Moniepoint / OPay / PalmPay | ✅ Strong | ❌ | ⚠️ Consumer only | ✅ |
| M-Pesa / Wave | ✅ Strong (non-NG) | ❌ | ⚠️ Lending share | ✅ |
| OmniRetail / TradeDepot | ⚠️ Retailer network | ❌ | ✅ Strong | ✅ / ⚠️ |
| MaxAB-Wasoko / Twiga | ⚠️ Eroding | ❌ | ✅ | ❌ |
| Yellow Card / Bitnob | ❌ | ✅ Strong | ❌ | ⚠️ |
| Noah / TransFi | ❌ | ✅ (infra) | ❌ | ⚠️ |
| Hurupay / Payd | ❌ (freelancers) | ✅ | ❌ | ⚠️ Thin |
| **White-space entrant** | **Borrow (Layer 1 agents)** | **Build/partner (Layer 3)** | **Build (AI underwriting)** | **Target** |

**No existing player checks all three of the first columns.** The incumbents are each strong in exactly one and structurally weak in the adjacent two. That gap — distribution × dollar-stable rail × AI credit — is the defensible position for a new entrant, most plausibly entered through *partnership with* Layer 1 agent networks rather than competition against them.

### Strategic Read for a New Entrant

- **Do not** build another payments wallet or another FMCG marketplace — both are saturated (one by profitable oligopolists, one by failure).
- **Do** build the stablecoin-credit-underwriting layer that rides on existing distribution. The cheapest customer acquisition in this market is the agent network someone else already paid to build.
- **Differentiate on** dollar-stability (the devaluation hedge), AI underwriting discipline (the OmniRetail lesson), and regulatory legitimacy (the Yellow Card lesson) — not on price or rails alone.
- **Watch** the regulatory wildcard (Nigeria's crypto stance, CBN onboarding controls) and the FX wildcard (further devaluation simultaneously grows the dollar-demand opportunity and compresses USD-denominated returns).
