# Can the "Bitcoin Treasury Company" Model Be Replicated in Africa?

*A deep dive into corporate strategy and entrepreneurial niche, with a Nigeria focus (plus South Africa, Kenya, Ghana, Egypt). Researched June 2026.*

---

## 1. What the model actually is

The "Bitcoin Treasury Company" (BTC-TC) playbook was invented by **Michael Saylor at MicroStrategy** (now renamed **Strategy**, ticker MSTR) in August 2020. The thesis: a fiat-denominated corporate treasury earning ~0% in a 2%+ inflation world is a melting ice cube, so convert it into bitcoin, the hardest-supply asset available. What turned a treasury policy into a *business model* was the financing engine bolted on top of it.

**The capital stack.** Strategy raises money through three repeating instruments and uses the proceeds to buy BTC:

- **At-the-market (ATM) equity** – selling new shares directly into the market, ideally while the stock trades *above* the value of the bitcoin per share.
- **Convertible notes** – low/zero-coupon debt that converts to equity if the stock rises; cheap optionality funded by BTC volatility.
- **Preferred stock** (Strategy's STRK, STRF, STRD, STRC series) – yield instruments that fund BTC purchases without diluting common equity as harshly.

**The flywheel (mNAV / premium-to-NAV).** The magic metric is **mNAV** — market cap divided by the market value of the bitcoin held. When mNAV > 1 (the stock trades at a *premium* to its underlying BTC), every dollar of new equity raised buys *more* bitcoin-per-share than existing holders gave up. That is accretive dilution: it raises **"BTC Yield"** — bitcoin-per-share growth. Strategy reported a **25% year-to-date BTC Yield by July 2025** (target later raised to 30%) and **>$13bn of "BTC $ Gain"** for the year. As of mid-2025 Strategy held roughly **640,000–845,000 BTC** across the year (it disclosed ~845,000 BTC, ~4% of all bitcoin that will ever exist). The stock becomes a **leveraged, regulated, equity-market proxy for bitcoin** — investors who can't or won't self-custody, or who are confined to equities (pension funds, 401ks), buy MSTR instead.

**The fragility.** The flywheel only spins while mNAV > 1. Strategy's premium ran to **2.6x–2.8x in late 2024**, compressed to ~**1.2x by mid-2025**, and **fell below 1.0x in November 2025**. Below 1x, issuing equity is *dilutive* and the engine stalls — the central risk of the entire model.

**The global spread (2024–2026 wave):**

| Company | Country / listing | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Strategy (MSTR)** | US / Nasdaq | Originator; ~845k BTC; preferred-stock pioneer |
| **Metaplanet (3350.T)** | Japan / TSE | "Japan's first BTC treasury company"; grew **1,762 BTC (end-2024) → ~35,000 BTC (end-2025)**; "555 Plan" targets 100k BTC by 2026; sustained a premium to NAV |
| **Twenty One / XXI** | US / NYSE | SPAC merger (Cantor); seeded with **42,000+ BTC** by Tether & SoftBank; ~43,500 BTC, 2nd-largest holder; Tether bought out SoftBank (2026) |
| **Semler Scientific, MARA Holdings** | US | Operating/mining companies that bolted BTC treasuries on |
| **Smarter Web Company** | UK | Smaller European adopter |

The pattern: a tradable equity wrapper around bitcoin, sold to investors who want exposure inside a regulated brokerage account.

## 2. The Africa thesis — why it might fit *better* than the US

The US version of this trade is, fundamentally, **insurance against a slow problem**: 2–3% dollar inflation. The African version is insurance against a **fast, visible, recurring catastrophe** — and that is the strongest argument for the model travelling.

- The **naira lost ~60%+ of its value** from ~₦460/$ to ~₦1,500/$ between 2023 and early 2025; inflation stayed **above 20%** through 2025 (peaking above 30%).
- Ghana's cedi, Kenya's shilling, Egypt's pound, Zambia's kwacha have all seen sharp devaluations and double-digit inflation. **Real interest rates are deeply negative** — local-currency cash and even local government bonds destroy purchasing power.
- Local store-of-value options are thin: shallow bond markets, capital-controlled access to dollars, property that is illiquid and lumpy.

In that environment, a hard-asset treasury is **a rational corporate hedge, not a speculation**. Africa Bitcoin Corporation's executive chairman Stafford Masie frames it bluntly: *"Africa's money is broken."* CEO Warren Wheatley calls bitcoin *"the perfect foundation for a strong African balance sheet,"* and the company's pitch leans on a real spread: African inflation averaging **~15%** versus near-zero local real yields. For a Nigerian or Ghanaian company, protecting shareholder value from local-currency collapse is an existential treasury question, not a CFO's clever side bet.

**But: BTC-treasury vs. dollar/stablecoin-treasury is the crux.** For most African corporates the *primary* enemy is local-currency devaluation, and the *cheapest, most precise* hedge for that is simply **holding USD — increasingly via stablecoins (USDT/USDC)**. Bitcoin solves the devaluation problem *and* adds a second, uncorrelated bet on BTC appreciation — but it imports 50–80% annualised volatility. For a business with naira costs and naira-or-dollar revenues, that volatility can *defeat the purpose*: a treasury meant to protect working capital can't be down 40% the quarter you need to pay suppliers.

| Dimension | BTC treasury | USD/stablecoin treasury |
|---|---|---|
| Protects vs. local devaluation | Yes | Yes |
| Volatility | Very high (drawdowns of 50%+) | Near-zero (peg risk only) |
| Upside / "yield flywheel" | Yes — enables mNAV premium, BTC-yield story | No — it's a hedge, not a growth engine |
| Suitable for *operating* working capital | Poor (timing risk) | Excellent |
| Suitable for *long-horizon* reserves / equity story | Strong | Weak (no premium narrative) |
| Regulatory friction (Africa) | High (crypto + FX) | High (FX) but more "boring" |
| Yield while held | None (or risky DeFi) | 4–7% on USDC in DeFi/T-bill-backed products |

**Honest verdict:** for an African *operating* company, a **stablecoin/USD treasury is the correct default hedge**; bitcoin only makes sense for the *strategic, long-horizon* slice of reserves — or for a company whose entire equity story is *built* to be a bitcoin proxy (the MSTR/Metaplanet model), where volatility is the product, not the bug.

## 3. The barriers — why it's hard in Africa (and brutal in Nigeria)

| Barrier | Severity | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **FX / capital controls (Nigeria)** | **Critical** | The single biggest blocker. You cannot freely convert naira → USD → BTC at scale. CBN restricts official FX access; naira-card international spend has been throttled to as low as $20–50; banks block forex-broker-linked transactions. Converting a meaningful corporate treasury would require either the official window (rationed) or the parallel/P2P market (legally grey, expensive, hard to audit). |
| **Banking access / payment rules** | High | Nigerian payment rules still **prohibit crypto for goods/services payment**; banks are barred from holding crypto-linked assets or taking crypto as collateral. A listco's auditors and banks may refuse to touch the structure. |
| **Thin, illiquid equity markets** | High | NGX, JSE-AltX, Nairobi (NSE) are shallow. The mNAV flywheel *needs* a liquid, premium-bearing stock to issue into. Thin float = no sustainable premium = no flywheel. |
| **Listing-rule permission** | High | Will an exchange let a listco hold its treasury in bitcoin? The JSE said yes (with conditions — see §4: 90% cold storage). NGX has **no precedent** and would face CBN pressure. |
| **Accounting (IFRS)** | Medium | Most African issuers report under **IFRS**, where bitcoin is an **indefinite-life intangible (IAS 38)** — historically cost-less-impairment (asymmetric: you book losses, not unrealised gains), unless you use the revaluation model (gains to OCI). This is *worse* for the "premium" story than US GAAP, which from 2025 marks crypto to **fair value through net income**. African CFOs get the ugly accounting, not the flattering US version. |
| **Custody / security** | Medium | Institutional cold-storage custody (Anchorage, Coinbase Prime, BitGo) is improving but adds cost; self-custody invites theft/kidnapping risk. |
| **Regulatory hostility / dollarization fear** | High | Central banks (CBN above all) fear that BTC/stablecoin treasuries accelerate **dollarization and capital flight** — undermining monetary control. This makes top-down approval politically fraught even where it's legal. |
| **Board / shareholder appetite** | Medium | Conservative African boards, pension trustees, and family owners are wary of headline volatility and reputational risk. |

**The Nigerian FX problem deserves emphasis.** Even with the ISA 2025 legitimising crypto-as-securities (below), the **gating constraint is foreign exchange, not crypto law**. A Lagos-listed company sitting on naira cannot legally and cheaply move that capital into bitcoin at treasury scale. This is why a *Nigerian-domiciled, naira-funded* BTC treasury listco is, today, close to **unviable** — the capital can't get from the balance sheet to the asset without either rationed official FX or a grey parallel market.

## 3b. Routes around the barriers

The workarounds all share one idea: **decouple where you raise/hold capital from where you operate.**

| Workaround | How it dodges the barrier | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **List offshore, operate onshore** | Incorporate the holdco in Mauritius, BVI, or Cayman; raise USD; hold BTC offshore; run African operations as subsidiaries | Loses the "local equity proxy" benefit; needs offshore investors |
| **Use a South African vehicle** | SA has the most mature framework: FSCA regulates crypto as a financial product (2022); JSE permits a BTC-holding listco (ABC). SA exchange-control is real but more navigable than Nigeria's | SA rand also weak, but capital account is more open |
| **Mauritius / DIFC / Dubai SPV** | A fund or SPV in a crypto-friendly hub raises USD/stablecoin from DFIs and HNWIs and holds BTC; African corporates buy units | Not a listed equity proxy; AUM model, not flywheel |
| **Raise in USD/stablecoin offshore** | Sidestep naira entirely; the treasury is funded in dollars from the start | Only works for companies with offshore revenue or diaspora/DFI backers |
| **Dual-listing** | Primary in SA/London/Frankfurt, secondary in Lagos/Nairobi to tap local demand | Regulatory complexity; ABC already lists on JSE, NSX, A2X, OTCQB, Frankfurt |
| **Fund / SPV instead of operating co** | A regulated fund avoids messy operating-company accounting and gives clean NAV | No mNAV *premium* flywheel — funds trade near NAV |
| **Tokenized equity** | Issue equity as a security token under ISA-2025-style regimes; reach diaspora capital directly | Nascent rails, regulatory uncertainty |

Africa Bitcoin Corporation is effectively running the **"South-African vehicle + multi-listing + offshore custody"** playbook — which is the most credible route discovered in this research.

## 4. Who is already doing it

**Yes — there is exactly one clear, verifiable pioneer, and it is South African, not Nigerian.**

### Africa Bitcoin Corporation (ABC) — formerly Altvest Capital Solutions
- **"The first African listed company to adopt a Bitcoin Treasury strategy."** Rebranded from Altvest Capital in 2025. CEO **Warren Wheatley**; executive chairman **Stafford Masie**.
- **Listings:** migrated from the JSE's **AltX (small-cap board) to the JSE Main Board, effective ~22 May 2026**; also lists on **A2X**, the **Namibian Stock Exchange (NSX)**, **OTCQB** (US), and **Börse Frankfurt** — explicitly a multi-exchange Pan-African expansion to bring BTC exposure to institutions. Planned reach into Botswana, Kenya, London.
- **Treasury:** started with **1 BTC as a "systems test"** in 2025; built to roughly **4.55–5.02 BTC** by late-2025/early-2026 against a stated **21,000 BTC long-term target**. Reported a **207% BTC Yield** off the tiny base.
- **Capital model — a deliberate MSTR/Metaplanet clone:** raising up to **$210 million** via **tranched, demand-driven equity issuance keyed to mNAV** (issuing when trading at a premium), plus a multi-class structure (ordinary + preferred A/B/C shares). **No debt** initially; gradual weekly/biweekly accumulation. It also runs an **SME-financing** operating business alongside the treasury.
- **Custody/governance:** Anchorage, Coinbase Prime, VALR, Luno; multi-sig cold storage; **JSE rule requires 90% of BTC in cold storage**; audited by Forvis Mazars. It explicitly cites **MicroStrategy/Strategy, Metaplanet, and the UK's Smarter Web Company** as templates.

### Adjacent / supporting players
- **Luno** (owned by Digital Currency Group; large in SA and Nigeria) is lobbying SA's National Treasury to classify bitcoin held by licensed exchanges as an **"onshore asset"** — currently it's *neither* onshore nor offshore, creating exchange-control uncertainty. That reform would unlock far larger institutional allocations.
- **Stablecoin/treasury fintechs** — **Yellow Card, Busha, Quidax, Bitnob, Borderlesspay** — are building the dollar-stablecoin rails African SMEs already use as de facto treasuries. (Busha runs a business product for corporate stablecoin holding.)
- **Failed cautionary tale:** **Mara**, the Coinbase/FTX-backed pan-African crypto startup, raised $23m, lost ~$16m, and collapsed/rebranded to Jara — a reminder that African crypto execution risk is real.

### Nigeria specifically
**No NGX-listed company holds bitcoin on its balance sheet, and none has announced a treasury strategy.** This is a genuine finding, not a search gap: the FX-control wall (§3) plus banking prohibitions make a naira-funded, Lagos-listed BTC treasury impractical today — even though **ISA 2025 (signed March 2025)** now recognises digital assets as securities under SEC oversight, and the SEC's **ARIP** has granted provisional exchange licences to **Busha and Quidax**. Nigeria has fixed the *securities-law* question but not the *FX* question. **White space exists, but it is white space because the plumbing isn't viable yet — not merely because no one has thought of it.**

## 5. The niche assessment — is this a viable opportunity?

Four concrete forms, assessed:

| Form | What it is | Viability in Africa | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) Listed operating co adopts BTC treasury** | An existing NGX/JSE listco bolts a BTC treasury on (Semler-style) | Low in Nigeria (FX wall, hostile CBN); moderate in SA | Niche; SA-only for now |
| **(b) Dedicated BTC treasury / holdco / SPV** | A purpose-built MSTR/Metaplanet clone (the ABC model) | **Moderate–good in SA / offshore;** poor if Nigeria-domiciled | The "pure" model — ABC is proving it, but premium/liquidity is the open question |
| **(c) Treasury-as-a-Service (TaaS) for SMEs/corporates** | SaaS + custody helping African SMEs hold **stablecoin/USD (and optionally BTC)** reserves, manage FX sweeps, earn yield | **High** — large addressable market, recurring revenue, lighter regulatory/volatility risk | **Most viable** |
| **(d) Stablecoin-denominated treasury product** | A managed USD-stablecoin reserve product (4–7% yield, instant settlement, naira on/off-ramp) | **High** — this is the actual hedge SMEs want | Strong, pairs with (c) |

**Opinionated verdict.**

**The pure "Bitcoin Treasury Company" (form b) is replicable in Africa, but realistically only via a South-African or offshore vehicle, and its long-term success is unproven.** Africa Bitcoin Corporation is the real test case. Its hardest challenge is not buying BTC — it's sustaining an **mNAV premium on a thin African float**. Strategy's own premium collapsed below 1x in November 2025; on illiquid African exchanges, a durable premium is far harder to manufacture, and without it the flywheel doesn't spin. Expect ABC to look more like a *closed-end BTC fund that occasionally trades at a premium* than a perpetual-motion compounding machine.

**The most viable *entrepreneurial* niche is (c)+(d): "Treasury-as-a-Service" for African SMEs and corporates, built on stablecoins first, bitcoin optional.** The reasoning:

- **The pain is universal and acute.** Every importer, exporter, and SME with naira/cedi/shilling working capital is being bled by devaluation. Stablecoins already represent a large share of African crypto transaction volume precisely because they're used as *working capital and savings*, not speculation. For a Nigerian importer, holding digital dollars is, as one source put it, *"a no-brainer."*
- **The model is a real business, not a bet.** Recurring SaaS/custody fees + FX-spread + yield-share (4–7% on USDC, swept to local currency on demand). It scales horizontally across Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, Egypt, SA.
- **Lower regulatory and volatility risk** than a BTC listco: you're selling *stability*, and ISA-2025-type regimes plus SEC/FSCA licensing give a path to legitimacy (partner with licensed VASPs like Busha/Quidax/Yellow Card).
- **Target customer:** import/export SMEs, fintech and e-commerce firms with cross-border flows, NGOs/DFIs disbursing in hard currency, mid-cap corporates wanting a treasury policy without a Coinbase Prime account. Bitcoin can be offered as an *opt-in, capped, long-horizon* sleeve — not the core.

**Risks to be honest about:**
- **Regulatory whiplash** — CBN has banned, unbanned, and re-restricted before; a stablecoin clampdown (dollarization fear) is a live tail risk.
- **Peg / counterparty risk** on the stablecoin and custody side.
- **FX on/off-ramp chokepoints** — the same naira-convertibility wall that blocks the listco model also raises the cost of the SME product; the business *is* partly an FX-arbitrage and compliance play.
- **Thin-float premium illusion** for any listed-equity version.

---

## Bottom line

The Bitcoin-treasury-company *concept* — a hard-asset hedge against currency collapse — is arguably **more rational in Africa than in the US**, because the disease (devaluation) is acute rather than chronic. But the *MicroStrategy mechanism* (a premium-bearing listed equity issuing into a deep, liquid market) depends on exactly the things Africa lacks: open capital accounts, deep equity markets, and friendly central banks. The result is a split outcome: the **flashy listco version is constrained to South Africa/offshore and unproven**, while the **unglamorous, genuinely scalable opportunity is stablecoin-first treasury-as-a-service for the SME and informal economy** — protecting working capital from devaluation, one importer at a time.
